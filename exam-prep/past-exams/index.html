    Section I — Multiple Choice

    Question 1

    [Module: M05 — Quantity Theory of Money]

    Consider a hypothetical economy producing and consuming only hamburgers. In this economy, the amount of money equals $200 and only 50 hamburgers are produced and sold for $4 each. Assuming a constant velocity of circulation, what would happen to the price of hamburgers if the amount of money increases to $400, other things equal?

    • a. Hamburger price will double.
    • b. Hamburger price will remain constant.
    • c. Hamburger price will increase to $10.
    • d. Hamburger price will fall as there are more dollars available.
    Show Answer

    a. Hamburger price will double.

    Using the Quantity Equation \(MV = PY\): with \(V\) and \(Y\) constant, doubling \(M\) from $200 to $400 must double \(P\). Original price = $4; new price = $8. Solution inferred from Quantity Theory of Money.

    Question 2

    [Module: M05 — Fiscal Policy and Crowding Out]

    'Crowding out' by government spending implies that:

    • a. Households consume less as they become more pessimistic
    • b. Private sector investment decreases due to an increase in government borrowing
    • c. Some people cannot find jobs due to an increase in government debt
    • d. Private sector investment and consumption decrease as the government size increases
    Show Answer

    b. Private sector investment decreases due to an increase in government borrowing.

    When the government borrows to finance spending, it competes with the private sector for loanable funds, pushing up real interest rates and reducing private investment. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 3

    [Module: M03 — Measuring Unemployment]

    Data for an economy shows that the unemployment rate is 5%, the participation rate is 60%, and 20 million people 15 years or older are not in the labour force. How many people are unemployed in this economy?

    • a. 1.8 million
    • b. 1.5 million
    • c. 1.2 million
    • d. 0.6 million
    Show Answer

    b. 1.5 million.

    Working:

    • Not in labour force = 20 million; participation rate = 60%, so \(\frac{\text{Labour Force}}{\text{Working-age pop}} = 0.60\)
    • Working-age population = \(20\text{m} / (1 - 0.60) = 50\) million
    • Labour force = \(50 \times 0.60 = 30\) million
    • Unemployed = \(5\% \times 30\text{m} = 1.5\) million

    Solution inferred from labour market definitions.

    Question 4

    [Module: M01 — Measuring GDP]

    GDP understates economic activity because:

    • a. Public goods and services are not included
    • b. It uses market prices to value output
    • c. It does not include non-market economic activity
    • d. The value of intermediate goods and services are not included
    Show Answer

    c. It does not include non-market economic activity.

    GDP only counts goods and services transacted in markets. Activities like household production, volunteering, and the informal economy are excluded, causing GDP to understate true economic activity. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 5

    [Module: M02 — Savings and the Government Budget]

    If household saving decreases by $4 million, business saving increases by $4 million and the government budget deficit decreases by $4 million, then private saving _ and public saving ___.

    • a. does not change; decreases
    • b. increases; increases
    • c. increases; decreases
    • d. does not change; increases
    Show Answer

    d. does not change; increases.

    Private saving = household saving + business saving = \((-4) + (+4) = 0\) change. A decrease in the budget deficit means the government is saving more → public saving increases. Solution inferred from savings identity.

    Question 6

    [Module: M02 — Real vs Nominal Interest Rates]

    If the inflation rate is 2% then the real interest rate cannot be less than:

    • a. 2%
    • b. 1%
    • c. 0%
    • d. -2%
    Show Answer

    d. -2%.

    Using the Fisher equation \(r \approx i - \pi\). The nominal interest rate \(i\) cannot be negative (zero lower bound), so the minimum real rate is \(r = 0\% - 2\% = -2\%\). Solution inferred from Fisher Equation.

    Question 7

    [Module: M02 — Real vs Nominal Interest Rates / Fisher Equation]

    Suppose that a country experiencing deflation decides to set the nominal interest rate at zero. We can then infer that:

    • a. real interest rate equals the nominal interest rate
    • b. real interest rate is negative
    • c. real interest rate can be greater or less than the nominal interest rate
    • d. real interest rate is positive
    Show Answer

    d. real interest rate is positive.

    Fisher equation: \(r = i - \pi\). Deflation means \(\pi < 0\) (negative). So \(r = 0 - (\text{negative}) = \text{positive}\). Solution inferred from Fisher Equation.

    Question 8

    [Module: M02 — Inflation / Bracket Creep]

    Suppose the income tax schedule in Australia is:

    Income Tax on this income
    \(0–\)20,000 None
    $20,000 and above 30 cents per dollar of income over $20,000

    Your income in 2022 was $30,000. Inflation between 2022 and 2023 is 10% and your pre-tax income rises between 2022 and 2023 at the inflation rate.

    • a. Your after-tax income increases by more than 10%
    • b. Your after-tax income increases by 10%
    • c. Your after-tax income increases by less than 10%
    • d. Your after-tax income is unchanged.
    Show Answer

    c. Your after-tax income increases by less than 10%.

    Working:

    • 2022: Income = \(30,000; Tax = 30% × (\)30,000 − $20,000) = $3,000; After-tax = $27,000
    • 2023: Income = \(33,000 (up 10%); Tax = 30% × (\)33,000 − $20,000) = $3,900; After-tax = $29,100
    • % change in after-tax income = \((29,100 − 27,000)/27,000 \approx 7.8\%\)

    The bracket creep effect pushes a larger share of income into the taxable range, so after-tax income rises by less than inflation. Solution inferred.

    Question 9

    [Module: M06 — RBA and Open Market Operations]

    Suppose the overnight cash rate is above the Reserve Bank's target. The Reserve Bank should respond by:

    • a. increasing its purchase of financial assets, putting the exchange settlement accounts into a deficit position that contracts lending on the overnight market
    • b. decreasing its purchase of financial assets, putting the exchange settlement accounts into a surplus position that expands lending on the overnight market
    • c. increasing its purchase of financial assets, putting the exchange settlement accounts into a surplus position that expands lending on the overnight market
    • d. decreasing its purchase of financial assets, putting the exchange settlement accounts into a deficit position that contracts lending on the overnight market
    Show Answer

    c. Increasing its purchase of financial assets, putting exchange settlement accounts into a surplus position that expands lending on the overnight market.

    When the cash rate is above target, the RBA buys financial assets (open market purchase), injecting reserves into exchange settlement accounts, creating a surplus and increasing the supply of overnight funds — pushing the rate back down. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 10

    [Module: M02 — Costs of Inflation / Redistribution]

    Suppose there is an unexpected increase in inflation. Then:

    • a. Borrowers and lenders are worse off by the same amount
    • b. There is a redistribution from borrowers to lenders
    • c. Borrowers and lenders are better off by the same amount
    • d. There is a redistribution from lenders to borrowers
    Show Answer

    d. There is a redistribution from lenders to borrowers.

    Unexpected inflation erodes the real value of debt. Borrowers repay in dollars worth less in real terms → they gain; lenders receive less in real value → they lose. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 11

    [Module: M02 — Inflation / Indexed Benefits]

    Suppose unemployment benefits in 2022 are equal to $300 per week. If unemployment benefits are indexed to inflation and inflation between 2022 and 2023 is 5%, then the change in unemployment benefits between 2022 and 2023 is:

    • a. $5
    • b. None of the other answers
    • c. $15
    • d. $0
    Show Answer

    c. $15.

    \(300 \times 5\% = \$15\) increase. The new benefit = $315/week. Solution inferred.

    Question 12

    [Module: M03 — Labour Market Equilibrium / Minimum Wage]

    The demand and supply of labour in a certain industry are described by the following equations:

    \[N^d = 400 - 2w \qquad N^s = 240 + 2w\]

    where \(N^d\) is the number of workers employers want to hire, \(N^s\) is the number willing to work, and \(w\) is the real wage (dollars per day). Suppose the government imposes a minimum wage of $50 per day. What will be the resulting change in (un)employment?

    • a. Employment will decrease by 40 while unemployment will increase by 40.
    • b. Employment will decrease by 300 while unemployment will increase by 344.
    • c. There will be no change in employment or unemployment as the minimum wage is not high enough to change demand and supply.
    • d. Employment will decrease by 20 while unemployment will increase by 40.
    Show Answer

    d. Employment will decrease by 20 while unemployment will increase by 40.

    Working:

    Equilibrium without minimum wage: \(400 - 2w = 240 + 2w \Rightarrow 4w = 160 \Rightarrow w^* = 40\); \(N^* = 400 - 80 = 320\).

    With minimum wage \(w_{min} = 50\):

    • \(N^d = 400 - 2(50) = 300\) (employment falls from 320 → 300, decrease of 20)
    • \(N^s = 240 + 2(50) = 340\) (workers wanting to work = 340)
    • Unemployment = \(N^s - N^d = 340 - 300 = 40\)

    Solution inferred from labour market model.

    Question 13

    [Module: M05 — Government Debt Dynamics]

    The government debt measured as a fraction of GDP will fall, other things equal:

    • a. All of the others lead to a fall
    • b. The higher the growth rate of GDP
    • c. The higher the primary surplus
    • d. The higher the inflation rate
    Show Answer

    a. All of the others lead to a fall.

    The debt-to-GDP ratio \(B/Y\) falls when: (b) GDP grows faster (denominator rises), (c) primary surplus reduces new borrowing, and (d) inflation reduces the real value of existing nominal debt. All three are correct. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 14

    [Module: M01 — Measuring GDP (Expenditure and Income Approaches)]

    The table below shows components of GDP:

    Component $billions
    Investment (unknown)
    Imports 170
    Indirect taxes 80
    Consumption 600
    Government spending 180
    Compensation of Employees (wages) 650
    Exports 180
    Gross operating surplus 340

    Suppose the estimate of GDP is the same using the income and the expenditure approaches. Calculate the level of GDP and deduce the amount of investment.

    • a. GDP is $910 and investment was $120
    • b. GDP is $990 and investment was $200
    • c. None of the other answers
    • d. GDP is $1070 and investment was $280
    Show Answer

    d. GDP is $1,070 and investment was $280.

    Working:

    • Income approach: GDP = Wages + Gross Operating Surplus + Indirect Taxes = $650 + $340 + \(80 = **\)1,070**
    • Expenditure approach: GDP = C + I + G + X − M → $1,070 = $600 + I + $180 + $180 − $170 → I = $1,070 − \(790 = **\)280**

    Solution inferred.

    Question 15

    [Module: M04 — Keynesian Multiplier]

    [Diagram described: A Keynesian cross diagram showing three PAE lines: \(PAE = 200 + 0.5Y\), \(PAE = 400 + 0.5Y\), \(PAE = 600 + 0.5Y\), with corresponding equilibrium outputs of 400, 800, and 1,200.]

    [Diagram required — Keynesian cross with three parallel PAE lines]

    The income-expenditure multiplier equals:

    • a. 0.5
    • b. 4
    • c. 6
    • d. 2
    Show Answer

    d. 2.

    Working:

    Each line differs by 200 in the autonomous component. Each shifts equilibrium output by 400 (e.g., from 400 to 800 when autonomous spending rises from 200 to 400). So \(K = \Delta Y / \Delta \text{autonomous} = 400/200 = 2\).

    Alternatively, \(K = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{1-0.5} = 2\). Solution inferred.

    Question 16

    [Module: M02 — CPI Measurement Bias]

    Which of the following are reasons the CPI can overstate the cost of living?

    (i) It does not take into account that consumers can substitute toward lower priced goods and services when relative prices change
    (ii) It may not fully capture improvements in the quality of goods and services
    (iii) The CPI includes investment goods and exports, which are not consumed by households

    • a. (i), (ii) and (iii)
    • b. (ii)
    • c. (i) and (ii)
    • d. (i)
    Show Answer

    c. (i) and (ii).

    Substitution bias and quality adjustment bias both cause the Laspeyres-based CPI to overstate the cost of living. Statement (iii) is false — the CPI basket is restricted to consumer goods/services. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 17

    [Module: M01 — Stocks vs Flows]

    Which of the following are flows?

    • a. Investment, budget deficit, income
    • b. Consumption, workers hired, capital
    • c. Investment, debt, income
    • d. Consumption, unemployment, household deficit
    Show Answer

    a. Investment, budget deficit, income.

    Flows are measured per period of time. Investment, the budget deficit, and income are all flow variables. Debt and capital are stocks (measured at a point in time). Solution not provided in source.

    Question 18

    [Module: M04 — Keynesian Consumption Function]

    Which of the following is correct as an interpretation of the Keynesian consumption function?

    • a. The Keynesian consumption function implies that your consumption depends on your overall wealth, rather than your current income.
    • b. The Keynesian consumption function states that as income increases consumption increases more than proportionately.
    • c. The Keynesian consumption function is consistent with the observation that consumption can increase even if disposable income remains the same.
    • d. The Keynesian consumption function predicts that if your current income is less than your expected future income, you should borrow today to finance your current consumption needs.
    Show Answer

    c. The Keynesian consumption function is consistent with the observation that consumption can increase even if disposable income remains the same.

    The Keynesian consumption function is \(C = \bar{C} + c(Y - T)\). An increase in autonomous consumption \(\bar{C}\) raises consumption without any change in disposable income. Options (a) and (d) describe the Permanent Income/Life Cycle hypothesis, not the Keynesian model. (b) is incorrect because \(0 < MPC < 1\), so consumption rises less than proportionately. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 19

    [Module: M04 — Types of Unemployment / Fiscal Policy]

    Which of the following types of unemployment can be best addressed using fiscal policy in the short-run according to the Keynesian model?

    • a. structural unemployment
    • b. frictional unemployment
    • c. cyclical unemployment
    • d. natural rate of unemployment
    Show Answer

    c. Cyclical unemployment.

    Fiscal policy works by closing output gaps. Cyclical unemployment arises from a contractionary output gap (Y < Y*). Structural and frictional unemployment relate to supply-side issues at the natural rate. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 20

    [Module: M01 — Business Cycles]

    Which of the following statements about the business cycle is not correct?

    • a. Growth recession is not necessarily a technical recession.
    • b. Unemployment is counter-cyclical.
    • c. Technical recession requires a fall in real GDP at least for two consecutive quarters.
    • d. Business cycles are periodic.
    Show Answer

    d. Business cycles are periodic.

    Business cycles are irregular — they do not follow a fixed, predictable pattern. The timing and duration of expansions and recessions vary considerably. All other statements are correct. Solution not provided in source.

    Question 21 — Real GDP and Inflation (5 marks)

    [Module: M01 — Real GDP; M02 — GDP Deflator / Inflation]

    The economy in 2022 produced:

    Item Quantity Price
    Bread 200 loaves $4 per loaf
    Milk 250 litres $2 per litre
    Eggs 600 cartons $5 per carton

    In 2023 the same economy produced:

    Item Quantity Price
    Bread 225 loaves $5 per loaf
    Milk 240 litres $2 per litre
    Eggs 650 cartons $5.50 per carton

    Part (a) — 2.5 marks

    What was the percentage change in real GDP between 2022 and 2023? Round your answer to the nearest decimal point.

    Show Answer

    Working (using 2022 as base year):

    \[\text{Real GDP}_{2022} = (200 \times \$4) + (250 \times \$2) + (600 \times \$5) = \$800 + \$500 + \$3000 = \$4300\]
    \[\text{Real GDP}_{2023} = (225 \times \$4) + (240 \times \$2) + (650 \times \$5) = \$900 + \$480 + \$3250 = \$4630\]
    \[\% \Delta \text{Real GDP} = \frac{4630 - 4300}{4300} \times 100 = \frac{330}{4300} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{7.7\%}\]

    Solution inferred from GDP measurement methods.

    Part (b) — 2.5 marks

    What was the inflation rate between 2022 and 2023? Round your answer to the nearest decimal point.

    Show Answer

    Working (GDP Deflator approach):

    \[\text{Nominal GDP}_{2022} = (200 \times \$4) + (250 \times \$2) + (600 \times \$5) = \$4300\]
    \[\text{Nominal GDP}_{2023} = (225 \times \$5) + (240 \times \$2) + (650 \times \$5.50) = \$1125 + \$480 + \$3575 = \$5180\]
    \[\text{GDP Deflator}_{2022} = \frac{4300}{4300} \times 100 = 100\]
    \[\text{GDP Deflator}_{2023} = \frac{5180}{4630} \times 100 \approx 111.9\]
    \[\text{Inflation rate} = \frac{111.9 - 100}{100} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{11.9\%}\]

    Solution inferred from GDP deflator method.

    Question 22 — Keynesian Model (11 marks)

    [Module: M04 — Keynesian Cross; M05 — Fiscal Policy]

    Consider the model economy:

    \[C = 8 + 0.8(Y - T)\]
    \[I^p = 50\]
    \[G = 20\]
    \[T = 10 + 0.2Y\]
    \[NX = 0\]

    Part (a) — 1 mark

    Determine the size of the multiplier for this economy. Round your answer to the nearest decimal point.

    Show Answer

    Working:

    Substituting \(T = 10 + 0.2Y\) into the consumption function:

    \[C = 8 + 0.8(Y - 10 - 0.2Y) = 8 + 0.8(0.8Y - 10) = 8 - 8 + 0.64Y = 0.64Y\]

    More formally, MPC = \(c = 0.8\), MRT = \(t = 0.2\):

    \[K = \frac{1}{1 - c(1-t)} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.8(1-0.2)} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.64} = \frac{1}{0.36} \approx \mathbf{2.8}\]

    Part (b) — 2 marks

    Determine numerically the equilibrium output. Round your answer to the nearest decimal point.

    Show Answer

    Working:

    Autonomous spending components:

    \[\bar{C} = 8 - 0.8 \times 10 = 8 - 8 = 0\]
    \[\text{Autonomous PAE} = \bar{C} + I^p + G = 0 + 50 + 20 = 70\]

    Wait — re-examine. \(\bar{C}\) (intercept) with the tax function:

    \[PAE = [8 - 0.8(10)] + 0.64Y + 50 + 20 = (8 - 8) + 70 + 0.64Y = 70 + 0.64Y\]

    Setting \(Y = PAE\):

    \[Y = 70 + 0.64Y \Rightarrow 0.36Y = 70 \Rightarrow Y = \frac{70}{0.36} \approx \mathbf{194.4}\]

    Part (c) — 2 marks

    Suppose that consumers become optimistic and choose to increase their consumption by 2 units irrespective of their income. Determine the new equilibrium income. Round your answer to the nearest decimal point.

    Show Answer

    Working:

    Autonomous consumption rises by 2 → autonomous PAE rises by 2.

    \[\Delta Y = K \times \Delta\bar{C} = \frac{1}{0.36} \times 2 \approx 5.6\]
    \[Y_{\text{new}} = 194.4 + 5.6 = \mathbf{200.0}\]

    Part (d) — 2 marks

    This question does NOT follow from (c). Return to the original model.

    The government gives a cash bonus payment of 5 units to households to increase consumption. To keep the government budget balanced, the bonus is financed by a cut in government spending of the same amount (5 units). Calculate the resulting change in equilibrium income.

    Show Answer

    Working:

    A cash bonus of 5 to households acts as a lump-sum reduction in taxes by 5 (i.e., \(\Delta \bar{T} = -5\), or equivalently autonomous transfer of 5). Effect on PAE = \(MPC \times \Delta\text{bonus} = 0.8 \times 5 = 4\).

    A cut in government spending of 5 directly reduces autonomous PAE by 5.

    Net change in autonomous PAE = \(4 - 5 = -1\)

    \[\Delta Y = K \times (-1) = \frac{-1}{0.36} \approx \mathbf{-2.8}\]

    Equilibrium income decreases by approximately 2.8 units.

    Solution inferred from Keynesian model — balanced budget multiplier variant.

    Part (e) — 2 marks

    This is an independent question.

    Suppose that \(PAE = 72 + 0.64Y - 2r\), where \(r\) is the real interest rate. Potential output in the model economy is \(Y^* = 200\). Calculate the real interest rate that will eliminate cyclical unemployment.

    Show Answer

    Working:

    To eliminate cyclical unemployment, we need \(Y = Y^* = 200\) (close the contractionary gap).

    Set \(Y = PAE\):

    \[200 = 72 + 0.64(200) - 2r\]
    \[200 = 72 + 128 - 2r\]
    \[200 = 200 - 2r\]
    \[2r = 0 \Rightarrow r = \mathbf{0\%}\]

    The real interest rate must be 0% to achieve full employment output. Solution inferred.

    Question 8

    [Module: M03 — Okun's Law / Output Gaps]

    An economy is experiencing an expansionary gap of 1%. Given that this economy's beta (\(\beta\)) is equal to 2, which of the following statements about this economy is most correct?

    • a. The actual unemployment rate exceeds the natural rate of unemployment by 2%
    • b. The natural rate of unemployment exceeds the actual unemployment rate by 2%
    • c. The actual unemployment rate exceeds the natural rate of unemployment by 0.5%
    • d. The natural rate of unemployment exceeds the actual unemployment rate by 0.5%
    Show Answer

    d. The natural rate of unemployment exceeds the actual unemployment rate by 0.5%.

    Working:

    Okun's Law: \(u - u^n = -\frac{1}{\beta} \times \frac{Y - Y^*}{Y^*}\)

    With \(\beta = 2\) and expansionary gap \(= +1\%\):

    \[u - u^n = -\frac{1}{2}(+1\%) = -0.5\%\]

    So actual unemployment is 0.5% below the natural rate → the natural rate exceeds actual unemployment by 0.5%.

    Price/quantity data:

    Quantity demanded 2018 Prices 2017 (base) Prices 2018 (current)
    Coffee 10 kg $6/kg $2/kg
    Tea 10 kg $4/kg $4/kg

    Consumers are completely indifferent between coffee and tea. If the official food inflation rate for 2018 is calculated using a Paasche index, substitution bias is demonstrated by the fact that the official food inflation rate is:

    • a. 40% while the actual food inflation rate is more likely 25%.

    [Module: M04 — Keynesian Model / Multiplier]

    In the 4-sector Keynesian model, all other things being equal, which of the following is most likely to increase the equilibrium level of output? (MPC = marginal propensity to consume, MPM = marginal propensity to import, MRT = marginal rate of taxation)

    • a. An increase in the MPC and a decrease in the MPM and the MRT.
    • b. An increase in the MPC and the MPM and a decrease in the MRT.
    • c. A decrease in the MPC and an increase in the MPM and the MRT.
    • d. A decrease in the MPC and the MPM and an increase in the MRT.
    Show Answer

    a. An increase in the MPC and a decrease in the MPM and the MRT.

    \[K = \frac{1}{1-(c-m)(1-t)}\]

    Question 21 — Keynesian Model: Multiplier and Equilibrium (20 marks)

    [Module: M04 — Keynesian Model; M05 — Fiscal Policy]

    Consider the Keynesian model discussed in class.

    \[PAE = (I^p + G) + c(1-t)Y\]

    Part (i) — 4 marks

    Derive the formula for the multiplier for this economy and explain in words what the multiplier shows.

    Show Answer

    Derivation:

    Set \(Y = PAE\) (equilibrium condition):

    \[Y = (I^p + G) + c(1-t)Y\]
    \[Y - c(1-t)Y = I^p + G\]
    \[Y[1 - c(1-t)] = I^p + G\]
    \[\boxed{Y = \frac{1}{1-c(1-t)} \times (I^p + G)}\]

    Multiplier:

    \[K = \frac{1}{1-c(1-t)}\]

    In words: The multiplier shows the impact on equilibrium income of a $1 change in the exogenous (autonomous) component of planned aggregate expenditure. Because each round of spending generates income that is partly re-spent, the total effect on output exceeds the initial injection.

    Part (ii) — 6 marks

    Assume that the marginal propensity to consume, the marginal propensity to save and the marginal rate of taxation are all equal in this economy. All other things being equal, if G increases by $390, calculate by how much equilibrium income will rise.

    Show Answer

    Working:

    Since MPC + MPS = 1, and MPC = MPS = MRT:

    \[\text{Let } c = s = t = x. \quad \text{Then } c + s = 1 \Rightarrow 2x = 1 \Rightarrow x = 0.5\]

    So \(c = 0.5\), \(t = 0.5\).

    \[K = \frac{1}{1 - 0.5(1-0.5)} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.5 \times 0.5} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.25} = \frac{1}{0.75} = \frac{4}{3}\]
    \[\Delta Y = K \times \Delta G = \frac{4}{3} \times 390 = \mathbf{\$520}\]

    Equilibrium income rises by $520.

    Part (iii) — 6 marks

    Explain in detail the mechanism by which a 2-sector Keynesian economy returns to short-run equilibrium from a situation in which \(PAE > Y\).

    Show Answer

    Mechanism (inventory adjustment):

    When \(PAE > Y\), households and firms want to spend more than is currently being produced.

    1. Firms find their inventories being depleted faster than planned (actual inventory investment < planned).
    2. Actual investment < planned investment (unintended inventory drawdown).
    3. Firms respond by increasing production to replenish stocks.
    4. As firms produce more, \(Y\) rises.
    5. This process continues — output expanding each period — until \(Y = PAE\) (planned investment = actual investment, inventories stabilise).
    6. The economy reaches short-run equilibrium at a higher level of output.

    [Diagram required — Keynesian cross showing \(PAE > Y\) below the 45° line, with arrows indicating output rising toward equilibrium]

    Part (iv) — 4 marks

    Explain carefully how, in the Keynesian model, it is possible for an economy to be at equilibrium while experiencing cyclical unemployment.

    Show Answer

    Explanation:

    Short-run equilibrium in the Keynesian model only requires that planned aggregate expenditure equals output (\(Y = PAE\), or equivalently, planned investment equals savings \(I^p = S\)). There is no requirement for output to equal potential output (\(Y^*\)).

    • If the equilibrium level of output \(Y\) falls below potential output \(Y^*\), a contractionary (recessionary) gap exists: \(Y < Y^*\).
    • Workers who would be employed at \(Y^*\) are unemployed at the current output level \(Y\) — this is cyclical unemployment.
    • The economy is simultaneously in Keynesian equilibrium (no involuntary inventory changes) and experiencing cyclical unemployment.
    • In the Keynesian model, prices and wages are sticky in the short run, so the economy does not automatically self-correct to \(Y^*\).

    [Diagram required — Keynesian cross with equilibrium \(Y < Y^*\), showing the contractionary gap]

    \[MRP_L = P \times MP_L = W \quad \Rightarrow \quad MP_L = \frac{W}{P}\]

    Growth Accounting

    \[\%\Delta Y = \%\Delta A + \alpha(\%\Delta K) + (1-\alpha)(\%\Delta L)\]
    \[\frac{\Delta Y_t}{Y_{t-1}} = \alpha\frac{\Delta K_t}{K_{t-1}} + (1-\alpha)\frac{\Delta L_t}{L_{t-1}} + \frac{\Delta A_t}{A_{t-1}}\]

    Solow-Swan Growth Model

    Capital accumulation per capita:

    \[\Delta\left(\frac{K}{L}\right) = \theta\left(\frac{Y}{L}\right) - (d+n)\left(\frac{K}{L}\right)\]

    Steady state (when \(\Delta(K/L) = 0\)):

    \[\theta\left(\frac{Y}{L}\right) = (d+n)\left(\frac{K}{L}\right)\]

    Savings-investment identity (closed economy):

    \[S + (T - G) = I\]

    Exchange Rates

    Real exchange rate:

    \[\varepsilon = \frac{eP}{P_f}\]

    (where \(e\) = nominal exchange rate, \(P\) = domestic price level, \(P_f\) = foreign price level)

    Open economy identity:

    \[NS + KI = I\]
    \[NS - I = NX\]

    AS-AD Framework

    • LRAS: \(Y = Y^* = Af(K, L)\) (vertical at potential output)
    • Short-run equilibrium: \(AD = SRAS\)
    • Long-run equilibrium: \(AD = SRAS = LRAS\)

    Aggregate Components
    M0 Hard currency in circulation
    MB M0 + bank reserves
    M1 MB + chequing deposits
    M2 M1 + savings deposits + CDs
    M3 M2 + larger deposits + money market funds
    Broad money M3 + other deposit-like liabilities

    Bond Pricing

    \[A(1 + r_{current}) = P(1 + r_{previous})\]

    (where \(A\) = price received, \(P\) = principal, \(r\) = interest rate)
